# Curio Bureau — Episode 002 Pitch

## Working title
**The Town That's Been Burning Since 1962**

## Logline
Beneath Centralia, Pennsylvania, a coal-mine fire has been burning since May 1962. It transformed a living community into a largely abandoned town. Explain how underground coal seams sustained the fire, why stopping it was so hard, and what the residents lost.

## First-three-second hook
**There's a town in America where the ground has been burning for more than 60 years.**

## Why this is episode two
The Samoa pilot proves Curio Bureau can explain human-made systems with strong documentary typography and layered maps. Centralia moves the channel to physical science and consequences, introduces more ambitious 2.5D cutaway and archival collage, and avoids making the first two episodes about unusual date rules.

## Editorial motion design beats (45–65 seconds)
- **0–5**: A normal Pennsylvanian residential street; the asphalt breaks into a cross-section. The camera tracks underground, revealing heat glowing inside a coal seam. Sharp, physical impact sound into ominous bed.
- **5–12**: Newspaper/archive treatment locks "1962"; an editorial map zoom moves from the US to Pennsylvania to Centralia. Show a real town map, not decorative random shapes.
- **12–25**: Interlocking 2.5D geological cutaways reveal coal seam, old mine tunnels, heat and gases. Animated heat-front diagram explains how the fire propagated. Use layered sweeping/rumbling audio grounded in the SFX library.
- **25–38**: 1983/1984 public intervention evidence; addresses and homes fade from the street grid as relocation numbers build. Keep human consequences central; no sensational faux victims.
- **38–52**: Show monitoring maps, subsidence, abandonment and road closure; transition cartographic markers into archival imagery.
- **52–60**: Dramatic visual release: the surface grows quiet while the seam still glows underneath. End on "The town emptied. The fire didn't."

## Factual lock
- Pennsylvania DEP says mine fire has been burning since May 1962: https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/mining/abandoned-mine-reclamation/aml-program-information/centralia-mine-fire-resources
- DEP official chronology lists relocation, coal-mining history, expenditures and continuing monitoring: https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/mining/abandoned-mine-reclamation/aml-program-information/centralia-mine-fire-resources/chronology
- US Office of Surface Mining Reclamation & Enforcement describes detailed 3D archival mapping of the mine network: https://www.osmre.gov/news/stories/centralia-project
- The initial ignition scenario is not universally established; state "most commonly attributed to" rather than assert an absolute cause.
- **Safety:** do not encourage viewers to visit the fire area; DEP warns of toxic gases and unpredictable collapse.
