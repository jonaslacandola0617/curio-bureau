==================================================
NARRATION PACING — HARD QA GATE
==================================================

Speech must never be mechanically sped up to fit narration into
a preassigned scene window. This rule applies to ALL Curio Bureau films.

If the TTS or voice recording exceeds the allocated scene:

- SHORTEN AND RE-RECORD the actual words, or
- change scene duration and re-time the storyboard.

Do not conceal bad pacing with atempo, time-stretch, pitch-shifting,
or an abruptly accelerated reading.

Aim for natural conversational documentary delivery at the chosen
voice's original speaking rate. Allow silent room for the score and
visual reveal where spoken lines finish early.

Maximum permitted technical tempo ratio is 1.045x, and even that
small change is not automatically acceptable aesthetically.

Hard verify every scene's natural recorded duration versus its
available narration window, including the 34–42 s and 51–58 s
regions that exposed obvious rushed-voice errors in the preceding
Centralia episode.

After any voice rewrite, REGENERATE actual word-boundary captions
and SRT from the final spoken recording. Verify both MP4 and WebM.

EPISODE 003 — THE TIME ENGINEERS TURNED OFF NIAGARA FALLS
==================================================

EPISODE:

THE TIME THEY TURNED OFF NIAGARA FALLS
Working short-form title: THE WATERFALL THEY SWITCHED OFF
Historical setting: American Falls, Niagara Falls, New York / Ontario, 1969.

THIS IS EPISODE 003. DO NOT PRODUCE ANOTHER SAMOA OR CENTRALIA FILM.

PRIMARY FACT LOCK (SOURCE-CHECK BEFORE RECORDING):

On June 12, 1969, engineers temporarily dewatered the AMERICAN FALLS
at Niagara, using a rock cofferdam between Goat Island and the New York
mainland to redirect the Niagara River's normal flow away from that
particular waterfall.

The American Falls remained largely dry until November 25, 1969,
when the cofferdam was removed and its normal flow returned.

VERY IMPORTANT DISTINCTION:

Only the American Falls were dewatered. The Canadian Horseshoe Falls
continued to flow, and received redirected water. NEVER imply that all
of Niagara Falls stopped flowing, that the entire river dried up, or that
the Horseshoe Falls were dry.

WHY ENGINEERS DID IT:

The operation permitted engineering and geological investigations of
erosion, rockfalls, and the rock debris (talus) accumulating at the
American Falls' base. The work was part of international deliberations
about preserving or altering the waterfall's future.

A later International Joint Commission study recommended against
interfering with the natural geological character of the American Falls
merely to beautify it; accurate timing and policy nuance must be checked
against the 1975 report, not compressed into a fictional instant decision.

The film should explain:

WHAT was dewatered: the American Falls, not the Horseshoe Falls
WHERE it happened: Niagara River, Goat Island, American Falls, Horseshoe Falls
WHY rockfalls and accumulated talus raised concern
HOW a temporary rock cofferdam redirected the river's flow
WHAT the waterfall and newly exposed cliff looked like during the shutdown
HOW geologists and engineers studied the exposed rock and debris
WHEN and HOW the water was restored
WHY the investigation raised a larger question: should people try to
preserve a natural wonder by changing it?

SOURCE-LOCKED EVIDENCE (PRIORITIZE THESE):

- New York State Parks 1969 engineering journal and exact June–November
  dates:
  https://nysparks.emuseum.com/objects/1817659/parks.ny.gov
- US Army Corps of Engineers retrospective documenting the cofferdam,
  studies, June 12 diversion and November 25 removal:
  https://corpslakes.erdc.dren.mil/employees/engineerupdate/Engineer_update_111999.pdf
- International Joint Commission 1975 policy and source documentation:
  https://ijc.org/en/preservation-and-enhancement-american-falls-niagara
- 1975 official commission report PDF:
  https://ijc.org/sites/default/files/Docket%2086%20IJC%20Report%20to%20Governments.pdf
- US / Canada 1969 agreement for the temporary flow diversion:
  https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1969-pt9/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1969-pt9-5-2.pdf

RESEARCH AND ARCHIVAL-ASSET RULE:

Gather rights-reviewed authentic before/after photographs, archival
footage where legally usable, official hydrologic and geological diagrams,
and primary documentation. New York State Parks' records and Niagara Falls
public-library holdings are promising research leads but DO NOT assume
an archive website grants reproduction rights. Record attribution and
license/permission status per asset. If a photo is unavailable or rights
are unclear, create a clearly labeled original explanatory illustration
instead of pretending it is a historical photograph.

SCENE-BY-SCENE EDITORIAL ANIMATIC TARGET (ABOUT 55–65 SECONDS):

0–5 s:
Begin with a genuinely compelling, detailed cascading American Falls
image/illustration with physically convincing flowing water. An upstream
rock dam moves into place; the roaring water dramatically disappears.
Switch from full waterfall roar to a brief meaningful silence and a
restrained low-frequency impact. Hook: "In 1969, engineers turned off
one of Niagara Falls' waterfalls."

5–13 s:
Map from North America to the Niagara River, then Goat Island, American
Falls and Horseshoe Falls. Map labels must be geographically meaningful.
Use exact visually distinct highlighted arrows to show which waterfall
went dry and which kept flowing; do not invent the physical geography.

13–23 s:
Show a construction-time-lapse editorial cutaway of the temporary
cofferdam: multi-part rock masses, river current and flow vectors.
The dam diverts the flow, not "closes a water tap." Transition the last
flowing water ribbon into an annotated river-flow diagram.

23–32 s:
Use licensed archival photos or carefully sourced original illustrations
to reveal the unusually dry American Falls face. Zoom to actual layers,
exposed rocks and talus, marked with restrained documentary annotations.
Contrast BEFORE / DEWATERED with matched camera framing where achievable.

32–42 s:
Explain the purpose through animated engineering evidence: cliff
cross-section, erosion, rockfall risk, drilling / rock-core investigation,
geological measurement, and archival reports. Show the problem one graphic
at a time. This is an analytical explanation, not generic dramatic fire or
disaster animation.

42–51 s:
Return to the cofferdam map and animate dam removal. Bring the American
Falls back with dynamic water compositing while the Horseshoe Falls
remains consistent throughout. Match the sound effect to water rushing
back, with a carefully controlled musical lift.

51–60/65 s:
Illustrate the longer policy debate. A historical 1975 IJC report enters,
the actual relevant finding is highlighted, and a thoughtful final insight
emerges: "To save a natural wonder, sometimes you have to let nature
change it." Finish with the Curio Bureau masthead. Keep the title entirely
within the vertical frame and allow a real end-card hold.

NARRATION GUIDANCE:

Fact-check each line and lock the voiceover before animation.
Narration must be a natural human-paced documentary read. If a paragraph
exceeds its available scene time, shorten the spoken copy or adjust the
storyboard rather than mechanically speeding up the voice.

Do not force narration to fill every scene. Leave natural music and
visual breathing room.

Hard check: no segment should require more than 1.045x tempo.
A 38-second or 55-second "suddenly sped-up" voice is a RELEASE FAILURE.
Regenerate word-timed captions whenever narration is changed.
Do not use fast-time atempo as a workaround.

VISUAL AND SOUND LANGUAGE:

Visual target is original Vox-inspired editorial motion graphics,
not a stylized low-poly environment and not a static infographic.
Use detailed composited moving water, real archival evidence,
geographically grounded maps, animated engineering drawings,
synchronized kinetic titles, camera continuity, paper texture
and document highlights.

Music should begin with tension, recede for the unusual sudden silence,
then rise for the restoration. Make the water diversion, barrier
construction, annotated river arrows, major historical photograph

from the connected Curio Bureau Google Drive SFX library.

Avoid fake historical audio; no archival label on generated sound.
