## Historical claims, dates and source links
1. NASA MCO launched 11 Dec 1998 and was lost on 23 Sep 1999; its mission was climate observation and Mars Polar Lander relay. NASA Science: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-climate-orbiter/ .
2. NASA board found ground Small Forces impulse file supplied lbf·s though the receiving navigation interface specified N·s. Ground model underestimated AMD thruster effects by factor about **4.45**. Crucially this was a GROUND software mismatch; do not imply an onboard flight computer used imperial units. NASA official report: https://discovery.larc.nasa.gov/discovery/PDF_FILES/mars_climate_orbiter_phaseII.pdf ; NASA lessons: https://llis.nasa.gov/lesson/641 .
3. NASA initial Sept 23 technical statement reported expected approach roughly **150 km** versus suspected roughly **60 km**, with estimates evolving later. NASA NSSDC: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/news/mco_19990923.html .
4. Spacecraft went behind Mars during orbital insertion as expected, but no signal was reacquired. NASA JPL original: https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-mars-climate-orbiter-believed-to-be-lost/ .
5. Process failures, verification and communication compounded simple unit mismatch. NASA safety case study: https://sma.nasa.gov/docs/default-source/safety-messages/safetymessage-2009-08-01-themarsclimateorbitermishap.pdf .

## Viewer-facing historical safeguards
- Do not sensationalize a photographed crash/explosion: no such footage exists and actual final fate cannot be visually established.
- Numerical comparison in the film represents schematic explanatory altitude paths, *not physically accurate to-scale orbital rendering*. Labels use approximation.
- Storyline: disappeared signal → nine-month mission → two ground units → factor 4.448 → underestimated small thrusters → wrong Mars approach → no reacquisition → systemic checks.
- The two units are **pound-force seconds** and **newton seconds**, NOT "miles vs kilometers".
