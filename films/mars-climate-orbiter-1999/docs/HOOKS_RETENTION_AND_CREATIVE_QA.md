## Three historically supportable hook treatments

**A — SIGNAL NEVER RETURNED (selected):** First image is the spacecraft at Mars's limb, approaching its expected occultation. Earthside telemetry awaits its return; the expected signal disappears. First voice: "In 1999, a spacecraft disappeared behind Mars." It is accurate to NASA's September 23, 1999 initial announcement: the craft went behind Mars, and its signal was never reacquired. No invented explosion or photographed destruction. Viewer question: why didn't it return? Visual engine: Mars occultation, spacecraft local motion and Earthside waveform readout.

**B — TWO UNITS, ONE VALUE:** Start with two physically different data interfaces receiving the same numeric payload: lbf·s and N·s. A thin connector tries to pass the number directly and a red contradiction expands by a factor ~4.45; then match to a tiny thruster. Voice proposal: "A missing conversion sent a NASA spacecraft dangerously close to Mars." Viewer question: how can a unit marker change a spacecraft's position? Accurate but first-frame visually more abstract, so not preferred for a broad short-form feed.

**C — PLANNED VERSUS ACTUAL:** A giant realistic Mars planet occupies frame one, with two independently anchored orbital-approach arcs, labeled expected approximately 150km and preliminary approximately 60km; the actual path cuts below the survival limit. Voice proposal: "The spacecraft was meant to pass a hundred and fifty kilometers above Mars. It came much closer." Viewer question: what bent the trajectory? Both heights are initial 1999 NASA estimates, not precise physical 4K-to-scale visualization. This introduces consequences fastest but may consume the quantitative payoff too early.

**Editorial selection:** A best balances immediate observable physical event, specific question and accuracy. No claim that public views prove A is higher-retention; user platform analytics remain unavailable.

## Causal retention ladder (nine distinct stages)
0: Lost contact already happening; promise why.
1: This was a nine-month interplanetary flight with a real science goal.
2: Data came from ground software; reveal imperial impulse labels.
3: Receiver expected metric impulse; display unconverted ~4.448 factor.
4: Small attitude-control thruster effects gradually misestimated (not a single spectacular burn).
5: Contrast intended ~150km with estimated ~60km Martian approach (1999 initial figures).
6: Spacecraft passes behind Mars; no signal reacquired.
7: NASA inquiry and systemic failure: safeguards could have detected the mismatch.
8: Consequence with evidence-aware final insight; never animate a photographed fireball.
