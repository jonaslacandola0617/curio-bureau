# Episode 005 — Three Hook Candidates

## A — The fluid contradiction (selected)
**Frame 0:** Already-moving authored wave of dark molasses overruns a detailed 1919 streetscape, under a period elevated railway.
**Spoken line:** This wave that tore through Boston wasn't water.
**0–3 seconds:** Main wave progresses, warm brown viscous bands shear, metal/carriage details reveal period; editorial words 'THIS WASN'T WATER' / 'MOLASSES'.
**Question:** How could molasses form a disaster wave?
**Payoff:** Roughly 2.3 million gallons escaped from a huge tank and industrial failure.
**Source:** Boston City Archives, January 2019 retrospective.

## B — The steel shell
Frame 0: a richly riveted steel wall stretches and splits. Spoken: 'One tank failure turned a street into a flood.'
Question: Why did the tank break? Payoff: engineering and neglected warnings.
Risk: abstract metal closeup less immediately comprehendible without context.

## C — Human scale
Frame 0: original anonymous editorial reconstruction of period street workers confronted by approaching dark wave.
Spoken: 'In Boston, people suddenly had a wall of molasses coming toward them.'
Question: Who survived? Payoff: human cost and rescue.
Risk: unsupported individually-specific human reactions; requires carefully designed reconstruction.

**Selection principle:** A wins for instant visual contradiction, but must keep the casualties respectful and avoid being treated as a comedy.
