# Episode009 – screenplay and emotional voice-direction proposal
Working title **THE ARMY THAT WASN'T THERE**. Designed around physical deception with an auditory payoff, no planetary charts, no giant repeated upper-left headlines. Runtime derived from actually recorded lines; approximately 65–82 sec target, only directional.

## Three distinct hook mechanisms
A. **Deflating tank reveal (SELECTED):** first frame an enormous realistic illustrated WWII Sherman-shaped tank moving into shot, then rubber seams and hose reveal. VO: "This tank was supposed to fool an entire army. It was made of air." Tone astonishment rising to confidential reveal. SFX air pressure deflation, controlled metallic fabric snap, near silence on "air" (designed sound, not real field recording). Artistic reconstruction of a decoy, NOT documented recording of a specific collapse.
B. **Silent battlefield / sudden invisible army:** empty twilight field then sonic drums and vehicle noise seems to raise the landscape from nowhere; voice "At night, the sounds of tanks crossed this valley. The tanks weren't there." Requires no bogus quote about a specific location. Wider historical inference.
C. **1,100 → 30,000 visual inversion:** large formation of soldiers ripples into canvas decoys and fake radio waves, voice "Eleven hundred soldiers convinced their enemy a much bigger force was nearby." Factual but first encoded frame depends on numeral comprehension.  A has strongest instantly legible moving object and unusual physical contradiction.

## Spoken narrative / direction map
Scene0 — Curiosity → delighted disbelief. Stress "tank", tiny breath, punch "air"; show solid silhouette physically revealing inflatable seams.
Scene1 — Context → confidential. "In World War Two, America assembled a secret force of about eleven hundred specialists." Stress "secret"; tactile radio & field props.
Scene2 — Visual lie → playful technical astonishment. "Artists raised rubber tanks and guns where an armored army should have been." Stress "rubber"; camera reveals valve, seams, tread and gas-fabric texture.
Scene3 — Sonic lie → anticipatory quiet then burst. "But a fake tank couldn't sound like an army. So engineers brought enormous speakers and played recorded troop movements." Pause meaningfully before "speakers". Ensure word-tied speaker cone vibration.
Scene4 — Radio lie → intent, clipped confidence. "Radio operators sent false traffic, imitating units that were never there." Sound of Morse-like signal—not claimed actual historical radio recording.
Scene5 — Same lie from every direction → controlled escalation. "Vehicle tracks, dummy artillery, sound and radio all carried the same deception." Linked detail planes without same camera zoom.
Scene6 — Historical payoff 1945 → stakes. "In March nineteen forty-five, they helped disguise a real Allied crossing of the Rhine." "They impersonated two divisions—roughly thirty thousand troops—with a unit of only about eleven hundred."
Scene7 — Witnessed result → impressed but measured. "It helped misdirect German attention as American soldiers moved across the river." Do not claim every soldier was saved.
Scene8 — Reflective coda → warm quiet. "The strongest army in that field wasn't made of steel. It was made of imagination." This is editorial inference, not a literal field quote. Final "Curio Bureau" into scene's field graphic.
