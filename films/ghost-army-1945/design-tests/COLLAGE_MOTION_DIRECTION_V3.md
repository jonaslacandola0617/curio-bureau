### BEAT 1 • 00:00–00:03.6 — “Made of air.”
**At 00:00:** viewer is already close to an original high-quality Sherman-like inflatable *illustrative reconstruction* with slightly raised collage tank silhouette, nearby torn newspaper strip, ripped cartographic base, folded masking-tape hinge, source-anchored yellow line pointing toward the seam. **00:00–00:01.35:** purposeful shallow parallax: a small diagonal dolly makes tank silhouette and stitched seam shift relative to distant map; stationary word fragment “REAL ARMOR?” sits on separate paper at right. Brief source-accurate held composition. Not a continuous sideways pan. Manrope spoken caption remains screen locked.

**00:01.35–00:02.45:** the camera STOPS, then the paper edge over the illustrated rubber seam lifts, exposing valve, nozzle, material thickness and stamped “INFLATABLE” evidence. An original animated hand-drawn contour line follows the seam with controlled boil; reveal is local to the source object; nearby ink highlight stays attached to its parent through any final easing.

**00:02.45–00:03.6 TRANSITION A (momentum matched-object cut):** the circular rubber valve begins a swift, *nonuniform* approach toward the camera. At the actual peak of screen-space motion the correctly registered opening MATCH-CUTS to a much larger vintage speaker horn aperture. The two different materials have different texture and depth; identical circle positions and directional speed create the continuity. A hard cut is intentionally hidden by correspondence—not a fade, slide, giant diagonal paper wipe, or travel of the whole archive.

**Decision:** selective parallax is justified only during first close inspection; stop it when the valve itself becomes the new information. Tutorial anchors T09 (image planes and repaired gaps) + T02 (drawn annotation) + T06 (peak-speed match cut). T06 offers a teaching example, not mandatory exact curve settings. This handoff is an original composition.

### BEAT 3 • 00:07.4–00:11.25 — “False radio traffic.”
**07.4–08.1:** the wave line finishes inside a detailed period radio face: own multi-part knobs, frequency numerals, worn metal plate, woven cable sleeve, label strips, actual source-local dial geometry. Recognizable archival styling without claiming genuine 1945 serial number text.

Signal pips appear in an **invented code sequence**, explicitly not real WWII intercepts. Per-character type appears only on *one* key two-word label, not every text piece.

**09.6–11.25 TRANSITION C (material-to-geography bridge):** a physical black cable springs slightly out from the radio's right edge toward the near foreground; while its thick paper mask fully occupies one narrow region, the camera changes view and the same shape becomes a **drawn river ink path** on an original map of the Rhine region. Edge registration is critical at the shared crossing location. The wire-to-river relationship is a stylistic metaphor, not historical causation. This is neither a constant-depth pan nor an unsupported claim the river is radio wire. If legibility suffers, replace with a simple hard cut preserving line direction.

**Decision:** no substantial parallax in Beat 3. Tiny object motion and legible graphic causality drive the scene. ## Design / asset separation contract
- From the four previously approved *static collage compositions*, extract or freshly author independently controllable **material elements**: paper base + repaired hidden pixels, torn overlays, depth-accurate tank body, separately masked valve/skin seam, ink marks, speaker array/horn/vehicle, radio body/dial/scale/cable, original geographic map, regional focus matte, labels and stamps.
- Flat AI images **must not** be presented as “layered” merely by slicing a rectangular grid. Each cutout needs semantic object mask, plausible paper-edge border/shadow, and sufficient hidden background reconstruction for the maximum camera range.
- 2.5D source group = coherent local space with layer plane positions, occlusion, and calibrated camera projection. 2D line/state transformations = correct source-local paths, no unnecessary 3D.
- Build the visual treatments separately: material/texture pass, halftone and paper grain pass, color print registration, optional localized edge boil. Finish only after compositions already look editorial without filters.
- T01/T10 finishing: subtle optional grain/exposure/jitter/chromatic edges, but no unconditional effect pile-up. T10 12fps Posterize Time is optional for hand-drawn line layers; actual movie still 30fps. No generic stock watermark, copied Vox icon, photographed source pixels or borrowed sound.

## Creative motion acceptance criteria — no substitute with codec tests
**REJECT** if each beat independently slides on/off like a presentation, if the camera travels sideways at constant speed all 16 seconds, if each beat repeats parallax, if the four scenes have separate disconnected backgrounds, if material layers move incoherently, if effects obscure the story, if label coordinates float away from source, if grain hides typography, if a cut causes velocity/position jump, or if the montage looks like the previous crude 2.5D demo.

**REQUIRE**:
1. First frame already has premium tactile collage art, including recognized hero cutout and material richness. Full beginning/middle/end frames retain polished art; no cheap middle-scene downgrade.
2. At least three discernibly *different* visual bridges: valve→horn speed match cut; speaker waveform→radio trace 2D path transformation; radio cable→river map contour/metaphor with material mask or deliberate fallback hard cut.
3. Selective 2.5D movement only when it reveals physically meaningful source depth; otherwise object-local motion, editorial cuts and held graphic reading.
4. Film has physically convincing paper cuts, genuine occlusion, source-attached annotations, no blank holes/overexposed cutout edges or unnatural pop-in.
5. Subject continuity: speaker/radio is not another random slide; original shapes carry visual logic into the next concept and story explanation.
6. Manrope centered captions stay clear in official caption bar; archival labels remain legible and rights/truth identified.
7. Voice/music/physical SFX matched to actual actions, listen on phone/headphones; no sound quota or whoosh on every transition.
8. Review at normal speed and scrub **actual distinct frames** (no 10fps samples interpolated to claim native motion). The study preview can be below 4K and labeled honestly; this is an art-direction proof, not a final full-film release.
