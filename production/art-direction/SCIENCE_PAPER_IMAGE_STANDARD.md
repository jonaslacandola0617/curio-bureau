# Science paper-and-image art direction

**Operational standard v1.0 · 14 October 2026 · Implements the owner's existing full-paper/image direction and current request for consistency.** Applies to new science films; existing approved films retain their approved appearance. This is Curio's chosen Vox-inspired visual language, not a claim that every Vox film uses paper collage.

## 1. Direct the visual argument before making the artwork

Write the viewer's initial belief, the sourced correction and the evidence that makes that correction visible. Then design connected initial, intermediate and resolved compositions. Commission assets only after deciding the camera inspection, image separation, source highlight and next-shot inheritance. Final speech sets event timing; never force narration into fixed page slots.

For each shot complete the [art contract](SCIENCE_SHOT_ART_CONTRACT.md). A photo is not automatically explanatory, and a printed diagram is not automatically visually finished. Every object needs an editorial job. Recognition, selection, comparison, explanation and reading holds are distinct actions.

## 2. Select images that can carry the explanation

- Find a cleared, subject-specific photograph or scientific image with the feature the narration actually discusses. Prefer a usable silhouette, informative crop and sufficient resolution over a merely attractive stock image. Record creator, source, item-specific rights, scientific context and intended use.
- Identify the exact detail the camera will inspect before choosing the image. Do not obtain an unrelated hero photograph and compensate with labels.
- Preserve aspect ratio. Record the largest on-screen native-pixel footprint of the selected crop. Compare it with the crop's available source pixels; replace, reduce the zoom or explicitly record the limitation if magnification exposes softness. A 4K container does not repair low-resolution source imagery.
- Grade for coherent contrast and print character while retaining information. Do not recolor a science image in a way that changes its interpretation. Label instrument/assigned colors, composites and reconstructions where viewers could mistake them for ordinary visible-light photographs.
- Separate photographed foreground, middle and background only when boundaries and the editorial purpose justify depth. Repair exposed hidden regions; keep those repairs contextual, never invent evidentiary features. If credible separation is impossible, use a directed flat print inspection or cut.

## 3. Compose a moving editorial frame

Make one clear first eye target. Use deliberate unequal scale and overlap: the current evidence dominates, supporting material establishes context, and background material stays subordinate. Choose the frame for the fact, rather than fitting every fact inside the same centered sheet.

An inspected photograph or source can reach or cross the viewport edges. Show enough of the whole source to establish identity, then move into the decisive detail. Allow the camera to discover information already present in the world instead of constantly spawning new cards. A relevant second source may be visible behind the first before its reveal.

Reserve the screen-fixed 42–58% Manrope caption lane while composing. Avoid hero/header/caption collisions at entry, overshoot, hold and exit. Headlines use actual pinned **Playfair Display 900**; size and line breaks fit the subject and available space. Do not put the same headline furniture on every shot.

Vary scale and treatment as knowledge develops: image recognition, source macro, detail inspection, printed mechanism, comparison and photographic payoff. These are options, not a required sequence. Reject three adjacent equivalent layouts without a meaningful developing state. A simple accurate printed figure can pass; visual complexity is not the criterion.

## 4. Give materials distinct physical roles

| Material | Designed treatment | Review failure |
|---|---|---|
| Photograph / photographic cutout | Image-specific contrast, preserved detail, intentional crop, coherent edge and contact/shadow treatment; optional plastic/print surface | Generic rectangle with unrelated torn border; duplicate silhouette left behind after separation |
| Documentary source | Authentic scan, or clearly labeled editorial excerpt/transcription; source-native text for real scans; convincing page margin and readable provenance | Invented newspaper masthead, false archival appearance, unreadable postage-stamp evidence |
| Scientific figure sheet | Refined printed lines/type, measured hierarchy, honest scale/legend, paper fibers and restrained ink interaction | Bare vector environment placed on beige only to claim paper style; decorative paths without explanation |
| Tracing / annotation layer | Clearly related to source geometry, distinct translucency when appropriate, source-local registration | Floating arrows, drifting highlights or unnecessary sheet clutter |
| Background paper | Visible fiber/print character, subordinate tonal contrast, coherent light and scale | One global texture asked to simulate every surface |

Paper edges follow material choice: clean print, trimmed clipping, folded sheet and torn scrap have different anatomy. Do not randomly tear every object. Match shadow softness/direction to the implied spacing; avoid the identical web-card shadow on every layer. Tape, staples and folds are optional editorial details, never required decoration.

Use the owner's paperFiber/plastic/photocopy textures by material role. Fibers should read at phone size; ink density belongs to printed marks; plastic belongs to a print surface when appropriate. Textures travel with their parent objects during movement. Inspect enlarged crops for tiling, stretched grain and mask-edge leaks. Layering must strengthen material identity without burying evidence or color distinctions.

Keep the approved `paper-image-v1` recipe strengths as the starting point. Tune only when a reviewed scene needs it; do not raise every overlay opacity to solve weak artwork. Preserve historical rebuild recipes. The locked endcard receives no additional finishing.

## 5. Present sources as evidence in motion

1. Establish the source and its attribution before extracting its meaning.
2. Bring the relevant page area large enough to read. Retain necessary qualifying context.
3. Draw a selective bright yellow/red highlight in the source's coordinates as the narration reaches the phrase. Accent must read as emphasis at phone size; muted brand gold is not the default highlighter.
4. Let the camera follow the highlight's progression, then hold the completed phrase. Compound blur and coordinated gradient exposure may isolate inactive surrounding material; keep the selected words and necessary context sharp.
5. Transfer the selected idea into the next photographic detail or printed explanation through an identifiable anchor or deliberate cut. Do not repeatedly slide a whole page in and out without advancing understanding.

Use real documents where available. A typeset excerpt is explicitly labeled as an editorial excerpt; a newly authored schematic is labeled when it could be mistaken for measurement or authentic source material. Never fabricate a receipt, article or research figure to fill a missing asset.

## 6. Explain science inside the paper/image world

Use a subject photograph to establish identity, then a printed figure or source-attached annotation to expose the relationship when useful. Preserve orientation, distinguishing features and units across the handoff. A diagram may simplify detail while preserving the exact features needed to understand the mechanism.

Scientific figures need deliberate line hierarchy, relevant labels and truthful geometry. Separate entities before linking them. Show input, path and consequence in a reasoned order; hold the resolved relationship. Mark schematic/not-to-scale, assigned color, uncertainty and reconstruction as applicable. Do not imply a schematic photon, molecule, force arrow or path is a measured simulation.

Do not alternate into freestanding vector worlds or generic drawn-object environments. Canvas/SVG remain valid tools for excellent printed figures, paper masks, diagrams and annotations. Applying beige and noise to a weak diagram does not satisfy the art standard.

Science topic selection currently favors a familiar observable phenomenon with a surprising, source-supported mechanism and usable real imagery. Topic appeal and retention remain hypotheses until channel analytics support them. No new episode is commissioned by this document.

## 7. Choreograph attention and finishing

- Give camera and object motion separate jobs. Parallax means independently moving, repaired layers; a single flattened zoom is a flat zoom. Use depth where discovery or scale benefits.
- Plan anticipation, decisive move, settlement and reading hold. Cut at actual registered peak velocity when the next move meaningfully inherits it. Do not automatically cut at the timeline midpoint or use that transition everywhere.
- Tie compound blur and brightness gradients to the selected object/source. A brief focus hunt resolves to a sharp evidence state; it is not continuous blur.
- Keep chromatic separation restrained around imagery; protect captions and selected text. Mechanical jitter, exposure shift and visible grain create film character without destabilizing comprehension.
- Apply edge boil/ink bleed locally to suitable printed strokes. Do not wobble photographic silhouettes or factual map boundaries until their meaning changes. Delivery stays 30fps even when local ink samples at a lower cadence.
- Verify full object exits, valid occlusion and repaired backgrounds at hard intermediate states. Blur cannot hide a clipping defect.
- Associate meaningful motions with audible appropriate existing Foley: marker, paper handling, camera inspection or transition as applicable. Avoid identical whooshes on every action. Preserve Bernard, evolving original score and independent listening review.

## 8. Mandatory art review before full animation and 4K

Select five genuinely demanding frames **after sequence planning**: opening recognition, evidence macro, hardest printed mechanism, difficult mid-transition/occlusion and photographic payoff. Compare at phone size and inspect native-detail crops. Review the hardest moving join as well as its stills. Owner approval of artwork alone does not certify motion.

Record each dimension as **PASS / REVISE / NOT REVIEWED**, with frame/time, evidence and the specific correction. All applicable dimensions must pass; do not average away a failed shot.

| Dimension | Passing evidence |
|---|---|
| Subject and factual identity | Correct recognizable source; honest figure/image labels; sourced claim and rights |
| Composition and hierarchy | Clear eye target, useful crop/scale, controlled overlap, no caption/title conflict |
| Material craftsmanship | Distinct convincing paper/print/ink roles; strong visible texture without lost detail |
| Asset fitness | Correct mask, preserved aspect, adequate crop resolution, prepared hidden regions |
| Editorial continuity | Explicit inherited idea; designed intermediate frame and readable resolved state |
| Motion and attention | Independent purposeful actions, stable source anchors, full exit, focus returns sharp |
| Audio integration | Appropriate audible material/action cue and clear narration/music balance in playback |

Compare against owner-approved Pig War R2 for directed depth, document inspection and audible actions; against approved London Bridge for photographic/paper integration and the exact ending; against approved Sunlight for the science paper/image direction. Approval records do not constitute a fresh frame-by-frame audit. An overall resemblance percentage is not a valid pass metric.
