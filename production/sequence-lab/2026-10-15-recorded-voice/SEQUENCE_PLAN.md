## What the five states prove

1. **Recognize:** establish one listening subject and the recording question.
2. **Connect:** two conceptual routes converge on a common hearing destination. Yellow means airborne sound; coral means internally transmitted sound. A photograph of a skull teaching model anchors the latter context.
3. **Capture:** keep that baseline and fork the airborne route into an actual microphone cutout. The internal route continues toward hearing while speaking.
4. **Remove:** switch the source to playback. The original internal route ends before the hearing destination. Its faint previous trace makes the subtraction comparable. The printed signal envelope comes from the approved narration’s 3.38–6.66-second passage; it is not a measurement of bone conduction.
5. **Resolve:** equal-scale ear photographs and aligned category rows compare speaking with playback. No original internal route is shown for ordinary recorded playback. Keep the microphone/room qualification in the explanation.

This is one developing visual argument, with an intentional stable comparison baseline in states 2–4. Recognition, branching, removal and comparison are distinct operations. The shared layout is justified by changes to the routes; it is not a requirement to repeat this arrangement in future videos.
