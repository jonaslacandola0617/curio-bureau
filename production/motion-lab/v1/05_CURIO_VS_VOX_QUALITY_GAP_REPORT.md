## The underlying cause is pipeline design, not one easy filter
The third-party samples often start with actual photographs, assembled paper textures and recognizable silhouettes. Curio repeatedly built code-level scene primitives before investing in detailed illustrations, then treated native 4K encoding as creative success. This creates a high-resolution container around visibly low-detail art. Introducing Fast Box Blur alone or a Roughen Edges preset will not close that gap.

### Three immediate production architecture repairs
1. **Asset design approval before motion:** require 3–5 genuine production-grade concept frames across different subject/rendering families. Include person/architecture/mechanism cards with multi-part detail. A picture cannot advance if the storyboard needs a primitive square stand-in.
2. **Hierarchical scene/camera and typography:** create an asset graph separate from absolute-time camera; local anchors for documents, traced arrows and shadow/blur/texture. Audio cue events assigned to claims, not arbitrary 8-second scene blocks.
3. **Reference-specific animatic QA:** annotate real source video segment, original mechanism sketch, screen-space motion and camera states, render only after source-based demonstration is approved. Compare motion, not merely screenshot color.

## Don't copy the wrong things
The samples' repeated yellow Vox logos, TikTok calls-to-action, specific newspaper stories and true photographs are **not our target identity**. Curio keeps navy/ivory/gold/coral, genuine Playfair Display/Manrope, editorial evidence accuracy and topic-specific visual metaphors. Reconstruct general technique from scratch.
