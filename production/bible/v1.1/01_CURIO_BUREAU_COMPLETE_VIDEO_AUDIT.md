## Executive creative diagnosis
Curio Bureau already has a recognizable magazine identity—dark blue, warm ivory serif titles, gold inflections, research markings, restrained drama—but its movies often resemble a **sequence of editorial templates** rather than a director-selected sequence of visual explanations. The sophistication of individual shots swings too far: Episode 007's lush opening feels like museum-quality illustration while its sewer and work-crew sequences resemble simple educational vectors. The most serious process flaw is that detailed style-frame quality was not enforced across the storyboard before rendering. A new palette will not fix this; a stronger asset standard, composition diversity requirement and story-specific motion planning will.

## Episode 001 — Samoa, 'The Day That Disappeared'
**Evidence:** recovered `Curio_Bureau_The_Day_That_Disappeared_Pilot.mp4` (51s, 1080×1920) and `Curio_Bureau_The_Day_That_Disappeared_REBUILT.webm` (~52.4s); NOT the exact named Vox Editorial baseline MP4.

**Timed observations of PILOT:**
- 0.0s: near-empty navy background and tiny header; clear brand but low scroll-stop force.
- ~0.4–2.5s: opening words *'A country deleted a day'* reveal incrementally alongside a small clock. Premise is strong **in language**, but visual proof arrives late.
- ~4–8s: static-ish large words transition into the 29/30/31 calendar graphic; calendar is the best understandable mechanism.
- ~12s: the phrase 'Never. Happened.' takes much of the frame but moves information slowly.
- ~17s: island map with Samoa and date-line context; credible informational direction but labels are extremely small in a 300px-wide phone preview.
- ~24–30s: twin clocks followed by date-line comparison; clear concept, stronger than the headline-only sections.
- ~40–50s: 'Still paid' text and restrained closing; informational ending, but editorial type dominates an otherwise sparse frame.

**Timed observations of REBUILT:**
- 0.0–4s: delayed title 'The day that didn't exist' on dark background; slightly more tasteful but still postpones the calendar demonstration.
- ~8s: ivory background 29 → crossed-out 30 → 31 is an excellent factual visual metaphor and best segment in both versions.
- ~12–17s: island/map location graphic; extended similar map treatment appears with limited visible progression at samples.
- ~24–30s: sparse date-line graphic and captions; useful concept but long visually quiet stretches.
- ~40s: 'Twenty-four hours. Gone' is strong final visual statement; final centered word card is less story-connected.

**Improvement:** make the absent calendar square visible **from frame 1**: a calendar snaps from 29 to 31 as 30 crumples or disappears, with 'a whole country' map marker introduced by 2s. Keep calendar-to-map morph, but retire empty navy title opening, avoid one standalone oversized serif headline per beat, enlarge map's useful geographic relationships.
