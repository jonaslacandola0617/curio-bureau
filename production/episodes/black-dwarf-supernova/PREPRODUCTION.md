## Research decision

The original editorial angle is the difference between increasing a load and decreasing its support limit. The main explanation is not a tour of every era of the universe. It follows one remnant and one changing relationship.

Primary scientific basis: [M. E. Caplan, Black Dwarf Supernova in the Far Future (2020)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.02296), especially §§1–3,6–9. Full six-page text inspected. [NASA star types](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/types/) supplies the white-dwarf context. [Author's university release](https://news.illinoisstate.edu/2020/08/black-dwarf-supernova-isu-physicist-calculates-when-the-last-supernova-ever-will-happen/) identified; complete page retrieval failed, so its unseen text is not used as evidence.

Scientific boundaries: theoretical far future; massive remnants only; proton stability required; timing approximate; detailed transient brightness and remnant outcome unresolved. Do not imply our Sun qualifies or depict a black hole as the predicted endpoint. The paper's §§7–9 support caution about the explosion itself. Do not claim a proven final event of the universe.
