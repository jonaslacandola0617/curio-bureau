# Sun disappearance — fact and representation lock

## Premise
Impossible thought experiment. The Sun cannot vanish; this is not a GR-consistent mass-removal process. Use the NASA pedagogical hypothetical to explain causal delay, not a physically possible prediction or simulation. Other bodies and the Milky Way remain; the tangent diagram is a simplified Sun–Earth model.

## Claim boundaries
- Daylight persists initially: illustrative consequence of existing sunlight in transit, not a real disaster photograph.
- Gold dashes, travel boundary and dual lanes: conceptual aids, not literal packets, measured fields or NASA simulations.
- An absence boundary spreads outward; no dark object travels inward from Earth.
- Gravitational propagation: changes in the field, not a claim that static gravity emits photons.
- Earth remains on its orbit during the delay; it does not instantly fly radially away or lose its own surface gravity.
- Departing path is tangent in an isolated Sun–Earth approximation. Label simplified/not to scale. No freezing/death timeline asserted.

## Existing narration
Imagine the sun vanished right now.
For about eight minutes, you wouldn't know.
This can't actually happen, but the delay is real.
Sunlight takes about eight minutes and 20 seconds to reach Earth,
so the light arriving now left the sun minutes ago.
Remove the source, and that last light still has distance to cross.
Then, daylight ends.
Here's the stranger part.
In this thought experiment, Earth's orbit wouldn't change immediately either.
Changes in gravity also propagate at the speed of light.
Once that change arrived, Earth would leave its old solar orbit.
So our first warning wouldn't be instant.
We'd spend those final minutes living in the sun's past.
