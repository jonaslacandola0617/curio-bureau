Working title: **The Dust Between Us and the Stars**

At one-fifth the speed of light, a speck of dust becomes an impact hazard.

Space between stars looks empty. It still contains gas and dust.

Now send a tiny probe through it at roughly sixty thousand kilometres a second.

In the probe’s frame, the dust comes at it that fast.

The danger comes from relative speed. Even a tiny mass can carry substantial kinetic energy.

On impact, that energy can heat and eject material, leaving a crater.

Repeat those hits across an interstellar journey, and the surface erodes.

So researchers study protective layers and designs that expose less area.

But the damage depends on the grains, the material and the route.

This doesn’t make interstellar travel impossible. It changes the question:

Can we build something fast enough to reach another star—and tough enough to survive the space between?
