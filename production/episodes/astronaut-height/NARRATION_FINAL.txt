Space can make you taller. But you don't get to keep it.
Astronauts can gain up to three percent in height while in orbit.
Most of that change happens in the spine.
On Earth, your spine supports your body's weight.
In microgravity, that normal loading is reduced.
The spine can straighten and lengthen, adding height.
NASA researchers have even used ultrasound aboard the space station to study the change.
It matters because spacecraft seats and spacesuits have to fit a body that changes in flight.
Back on Earth, astronauts return to their normal height.
So this isn't a permanent growth spurt.
It's a temporary change in your spine.
