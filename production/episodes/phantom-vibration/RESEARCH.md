Claim C1: Feeling a mobile device vibration when it did not vibrate is a documented reported experience. S1 definition, corroborated S2. Depict an explicitly illustrative scenario, not an observed participant.
Claim C2: Expectation influencing interpretation of another sensation is a proposed explanation. S1 discussion; NOT experimentally established cause. Narration says “one proposed explanation” and “may”; retain visible “PROPOSED EXPLANATION” during the mechanism passage.
Claim C3: Clothing pressure and muscle contractions are candidate sensations, not proven universal triggers. S1 discussion. All representations marked possible; do not depict a measured neurological pathway or fabricate brain scans.
Claim C4: Survey evidence establishes reports and associations, not the causal mechanism. S1 cross-sectional self-report design and limitations. Do not claim a general-population prevalence from the medical sample.
Claim C5: In S1,115 of169 respondents answering the experience question reported it. A visual may show this EXACT sample denominator with “medical staff / one institution / self-report / 2010”; not 115 of176 total respondents, not “68% of everyone.” Current narration omits the number for pace.
S2 reports89% of290 undergraduates in its sample; context only, NOT planned narration or a broad population claim.

Important distinctions:
- An empty notifications screen alone does NOT establish a phantom vibration. Real vibrations may occur without a retained notification. Opening is a constructed example whose narration says “sometimes.”
- No claim of phone addiction, brain damage, dopamine depletion, proof of a mental disorder, electromagnetic muscle stimulation or treatment.
- Never animate the actual phone shaking while claiming the physical device was still. A separate printed annotation denotes the FELT sensation, then is contrasted with device state.
- Feeling a sensation is not the same as demonstrating its physical source.
- Final phrasing is restricted to a phantom-vibration scenario, not every unexplained bodily sensation.
