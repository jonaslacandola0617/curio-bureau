# Sunlight: claim and rights lock
Verified 13 October 2026. All narration claims paraphrase primary NASA explanations. No claim of proprietary Vox equivalence.

## Scientific claims
- Visible sunlight is a blend of colors and appears white: NASA Space Place, https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/blue-sky/en/ . The exact excerpt printed is eight words: “The light from the Sun looks white.” Typeset by Curio and labeled SOURCE EXCERPT / EDITORIAL TRANSCRIPTION. Surrounding paper wording is our paraphrase; no authentic scan is claimed.
- A prism separates visible colors; air molecules scatter blue more strongly than red; low Sun means a longer atmospheric path and warmer direct light: same NASA Space Place source. This is a simplified explanation, not a calculation of the full solar spectrum, color perception or atmospheric radiative transfer. Other solar wavelengths exist; visible colors are scoped explicitly.
- Some scientific solar imagery assigns colors to instrument wavelengths, including invisible ones: NASA Goddard, Jewel Box Sun, https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/11385/ and https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/solar-dynamics-observatory-shows-suns-rainbow-of-wavelengths/ . The instrument print remains labeled ASSIGNED COLORS. No natural-color claim is attached to it.
