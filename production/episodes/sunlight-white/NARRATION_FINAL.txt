Why does the Sun look yellow, when its light is white?
Sunlight is a blend of visible colors. Together, that blend looks white.
A prism separates them into a rainbow.
Before that light reaches you, it passes through Earth's atmosphere.
Air molecules scatter shorter wavelengths, like blue, more strongly than red. That scattered blue light gives us a blue sky.
When the Sun is low, light travels through more air. More blue is scattered out of the direct beam, leaving warmer colors.
That's why sunsets look golden, orange, or red.
And some colorful solar images use assigned colors to show wavelengths we can't see.
So the star hasn't changed. The air, and how we image it, change what we see.
