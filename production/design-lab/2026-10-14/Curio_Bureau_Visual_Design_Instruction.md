# Curio Bureau — image-led editorial design instruction

**Review module v0.1 · 14 October 2026 UTC.** Infrastructure proposal for the current static-art lab. The owner’s requested gate—approve four static visuals before animation—is binding now. The four designs and their experimental palettes remain unapproved. Current Bible v1.4.3, image-led direction, approved films, audio, captions and exact ending remain intact.

## Design responsibility

The designer must make the factual idea visible through image choice and arrangement. Beautiful image sourcing, confident typography and tactile surface craft are separate obligations from motion quality. A moving attractive photograph with no explanatory relationship is incomplete. A clever explanatory frame with generic or poorly integrated imagery is also incomplete.

Design in this order: proposition and evidence → visual alternatives → intended recognition/inference states → crop/layer requirements → final static art → owner review → authorized moving test. Plan the relationship to the next shot before art, even when only the static state is currently implemented. This strengthens, rather than reverses, the adopted sequence-first principle.

## 1. Source-specific visual premise

Every frame needs a one-sentence visual premise. Examples: “The ear and microphone represent different listening routes”; “an astronaut context surrounds a labeled model of an unloaded spine”; “the photographed prism makes colour separation observable”; “a missing print fragment is a metaphor for information the brain fills in.” None is a complete mechanism demonstration on its own.

Record which elements establish identity, which carry evidence and which are contextual or metaphorical. Prefer actual photographs and independently prepared photographic cutouts for the important objects. Paper is backing, print, source or selection support. Refined source-attached diagrams remain available when needed; they must not replace difficult photographic work with generic shapes.

## 2. Composition contract

For each key frame record:

| Decision | Required answer |
|---|---|
| Proposition | What accurate understanding should change? |
| First eye target | Which image feature earns the largest contrast/scale? |
| Secondary discovery | Which relation rewards a second look? |
| Image operation | Crop, juxtapose, repeat, subtract, inspect or compare—and why? |
| Quiet region | Where can the eye rest and captions later read? |
| Image/type relation | Does type sit beside, intersect, reveal or frame the subject? |
| Material | What physical surface is implied by each layer? |
| Truth boundary | What must not be inferred from this composition? |
| Future state | What would change next, and what remains recognizable? |

Create at least two rough composition alternatives for difficult facts. Select for meaning and subject identity, then refine. Do not choose a layout merely because its animation helper already exists.

## 3. Photographic craft

Use deliberate crop and unequal scale. Inspect the subject before clipping; preserve the feature that identifies it. A silhouette can cross the frame edge, but a useful face, mechanism, spectrum or joint should not disappear accidentally. Keep aspect ratios and source identity intact.

Masks follow meaningful contours. Inspect hair, thin components and translucent edges against both light and dark surfaces. Use actual separation or an intentional image fragment; arbitrary polygon clipping is not a substitute for a complex semantic cutout. Repair hidden areas before motion exposes them. Repairs must never invent evidentiary features.

Treat print borders, silhouette backings, seams and contact shadows as different material constructions. Use shadows to establish distance and contact. Keep one coherent lighting direction within a physical stack. Avoid one identical drop shadow and torn border on every image.

Preserve full-resolution originals. Log the pixel dimensions of the selected crop and compare them with its largest native delivery footprint. Browser-compressed study assets are previews. Where detail cannot support the planned close-up, choose a better source or reduce the footprint; a 4K canvas cannot repair it.

## 4. Typography and colour

Editorial titles use the pinned Playfair Display 900. Functional labels use Manrope. Type should be optically composed, not stretched to fit. Inspect actual loaded glyphs, line breaks, margins and overlaps. Large type is purposeful; not every frame requires a huge three-line heading.

One subject-owned dominant palette and one meaningful attention accent are sufficient for most compositions. Preserve category colours through comparison states. Bright yellow/red selection is appropriate when it identifies important evidence. Scientific colours remain faithful to their meaning.

Lab palettes are review proposals. Do not silently replace Curio’s brand tokens with universal orange, acid yellow or monochrome because one study looks good. Transfer source-specific design decisions rather than a universal Vox preset.

## 5. Materials with distinct jobs

Use existing owner textures first. Attach substrate fibre to backing, print/photocopy density to ink and photographed objects, and plastic highlights to a plausible covering. Complementary materials may stack, but each must have a reason and controlled extent.

Check effects on/off at phone scale. Visible texture should enrich the surface without burying the decisive photographic detail. Tune scale, tonal contrast and local blending before adding another effect. Do not texture the locked ending differently.

Film jitter, exposure variation, grain cadence, compound focus blur, focus hunts, chromatic separation, ink bleed, edge boil and parallax retain their existing contracts. They are later motion tools, not substitutes for static design quality. This lab implements no temporal effects.

## 6. Avoid the new template trap

Reject a batch whose artwork reduces to the same heading, central photo card and footnote with changed subject matter. Check silhouettes and eye paths with headings hidden. Vary how imagery carries meaning: organic crop, source-dominant print, specimen field, full-bleed interruption, accurate comparison or meaningful repetition. These are possible families, not a mandatory recipe.

The four lab studies are independent tests, not four consecutive shots or future episode templates. Do not reuse the astronaut/Earth arrangement for unrelated topics. Repetition is appropriate only when a common reference frame clarifies a changed fact.

## 7. Static gate and motion handoff

Review each dimension independently: factual/image integrity, subject specificity, composition, image integration, typography, material craft, phone clarity and layer readiness. Use OWNER PASS / REVISE / NOT REVIEWED, with reasons; never claim artistic approval from a browser check.

Stop after presenting the four static boards. After owner approval, design the actual narrative sequence and animate a short hard passage. Review recognition, intermediate composition, moving mask/occlusion, source-local annotations, caption placement, full exits, reading holds and audible action cues independently. Do not animate a flattened poster with a global zoom and call the integration successful.

The caption guide marks 42–58%; it does not certify a final caption layout. These static compositions may need reframing, staged type removal or camera separation before a voiced film. Body/science qualifiers near the bottom must be relocated or conveyed in the voiced sequence before posting; the static study is not upload-ready.

## 8. Efficient checkpoint workflow

Save once per meaningful milestone: source audit and concept choice; artwork/source package; owner approval; moving preflight; final render sections and mix. Keep code/docs and cleared metadata in git. Keep restricted material and complete private bundles out of public git. Retain originals separately from delivery-compressed assets. Reuse successful technical checks unless inputs change. Spend iteration on the weak composition instead of repeatedly generating voice, music or effects.

## Evidence basis

The source audit separates direct visual observation from creator reports and Curio proposals. The observed creator work supports deliberate photographic fragments, subject-specific visual motifs, restrained semantic colour and crafted material integration. Adobe adds practical masking/collage craft; the Domestika public outline supports brief-to-composition sequencing. These sources do not establish proprietary Vox settings, a universal visual style, or a measurable retention gain.
