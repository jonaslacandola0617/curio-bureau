## 6. SCENE GRAPH, LAYERED CAMERA AND ACTUAL ANIMATION ENGINEERING

Each animated object exists in its **own local coordinates**, inherits independently controlled parent transforms and may carry linked masks/labels and animations. Cameras are separate timeline objects from scene content. Avoid `ctx.drawImage(sceneBackground)` plus globally scaling everything as a proxy for sophisticated 2.5D direction.

Minimum deterministic shot schema:

```javascript
// Conceptual schema, not a complete runnable renderer.
Episode {
  width:2160, height:3840, fps:30,
  voiceMaster, wordAlignment, soundCues, factualSources,
  shotLedger: [ Shot {
    id, claimId, narrationWordRange, timeStart, timeEnd,
    camera: { perspective, target, keyframes, easing },
    layers: [ Node {
      id, parentId, assetRef, zDepth, localTransformKeys,
      maskId, anchorPoints, grainSpace, material, sourceId
    }],
    annotations: [{parentAssetId, localCoordinates, revealTimes}],
    captions: {screenSpace:true, region:[0.42,0.58]},
    visualStateBeats, sfxLinks, nextShotMatchObject,
    compositionFingerprint, qaTests
  }]
}
```

2D parent transforms: `M_world(node,t)=M_world(parent,t) × M_local(node,t)`; camera then maps world to screen: `P(t) × V(t) × M_world(node,t)`. Preserve object/page-space local highlights through camera motion while narration captions remain screen-space overlays. Use z-aware parallax or authored 2.5D plane transforms; proper occlusion/masks, not identical affine movement across every layer.

Expose deterministic controls `setTime(seconds)`, `setFrame(frame)`, `renderFrame(frame)`, `getDuration()`, `getFPS()`; no dependence on accumulated `requestAnimationFrame` delta or live timer for exports. Browser-openable editor with Play/Pause/Replay/Scrub, event links, layer inspection, captions safe guide, and WebM export where supported. Primary MP4 comes from real FFmpeg-backed native frame pipeline, not a fake download button.

**Timeline derives from audio:** lock waveform onsets/offsets first. Assign camera, masks, structural movement, data reveals and Foley to claim/word cue IDs. Support easing diversity: step/hold, cubic ease, physically plausible slow mechanisms, restrained overshoot when justified, path stroke reveals, stagger and masking. Distinguish source object motion from camera travel by checking transformed anchors across adjacent frames.

**Pre-render motion evidence:** produce 2–5s moving **animatic micro-studies for the project's hardest shots** as production preflight when video production is explicitly authorized. These are original development previews, not copied tutorial sequences or an extra public film. Approval requires moving frames and UI-safe captions where relevant. If only a beautiful still is shown, the motion skill is NOT validated.

Audition the credible best choice in context. The producer must explain why an effect's sonic material matches the depicted scale and causal action. **Newer SFX packs are resources, not a quota**. Don't fire whooshes simply because the script moved to the next line.

### 8C. SOUND-LED HOOKS AND SCENE-SPECIFIC EMOTIONAL DIRECTION

The opening first **0–5 seconds** is a combined audiovisual problem: compelling real visual consequence, an actor-like vocal reaction, acoustic space, and one well-placed cue or intentional hush. Audition at least **two actual mixed hook treatments** at normal phone loudness before animation investment. A fixed bass drop on the first frame is not an automatically strong hook. If the first frame shows real machinery moving, a truthful physical source may carry more suspense than a cinematic boom.

Design **a sonic continuity arc** to match this particular topic (example stages, not fixed time slots):
- Contradiction appears: either an immediately legible physical effect or a short restrained information accent.
- Unanswered question: narrowed sound field and carefully voiced curiosity, giving key words clean space.
- Investigation/mechanism: distinctive material/operational effects attached to independent moving components (one latch should sound different from an entire factory); prioritize physical synchronization at ±1–2 frames where appropriate.
- Complication: musical harmonic shift, purposeful drop into low ambience, or a rare high-impact cue; vary it from the prior three films.
- Payoff: musical resolution, emotional speaker landing and 0.8–1.8s closing without padding.

Create an **audio emotional-trajectory sheet** charting *narration energy, music tension, environmental intensity and SFX emphasis* in time; choose useful contrast instead of making all tracks high-energy throughout. In tragic stories, respect tone rather than sensationalizing suffering.

### 8D. AUDIO-BEFORE-PICTURE APPROVAL, MIX, AND LISTENING TESTS

Hard preflight before complete 4K render: produce a **5–10-second Bernard AUDIO + draft-image hook** and at least one **mechanism/twist audio vignette** with actual chosen narration, rhythmic/music cues and at least two deliberately timed context-relevant sound details where the story supports them. Listen and select the strongest variant. If human listening access is unavailable, preserve candidates and label creative audio approval open; DO NOT call it final-ready.

Maintain separate stems/buses: `VOICE / MUSIC / AMBIENCE / PHYSICAL FOLEY / EDITORIAL INFORMATION / TRANSITIONS / SUB-IMPACTS / MASTER`. The `audio_cue_sheet.csv` schema MUST include `cue_id, exact file path, SHA256, source provider, license/rights status, real-vs-designed flag, narrative_word_id, animation_event_id, cue_onset_s, duration_s, bus, target_gain, automation/ducking, pan, source rationale, final audible-check verdict`. For major shots, also track `narration_performance_map`, `music_transition_event` and `sfx_sync_frame`. Include recorded Foley only where the licensing status permits the intended platform and distribution; do not copy third-party raw files into a public code repo.

QA must verify: first word clearly audible; no clipped consonants; speech expressively shaped; musical themes and story shifts really audible; specific physical effects synchronized with moving parts; ambience creates place without masking voice; impact sounds not overused; no annoying repeated UI bleeps; lossless stems and actual encoded MP4 and WebM match; final mix assessed on **mobile speakers and headphones**. Integrated loudness near −14 to −12 LUFS and ≤−1 dBTP are useful measurements **only**, not the verdict. If the actual sound quality remains flat, weak, overly synthetic, fake-historical, or crowded, mark **CREATIVE AUDIO QA FAIL** and revise even when the video technically exports.

### 8E. AUDIO ANTI-REPETITION AND EPISODE-SPECIFIC SOUND IDENTITY

Keep a `sound_fingerprint.csv`: `scene_id, lead_sound_material, musical_motif, SFX_categories, major_reveal_cue, ambience_source, voice_emotion, start/end spectral texture, recent_episode_similarity, editorial_reason`. Review against Episodes 006, 007 and 008. Avoid the same robotic bleep, same bass drop, same short whoosh and same identical dramatic rise on every mystery. Channel consistency lives in taste, dynamics, balance and physical synchronization; timbre and instrumentation should match each story.

**Perceived engagement is a creative outcome, not a sound-count target.** Better narration can carry an almost silent frame; a compelling mechanical montage can need multiple small physical events. Do not mandate arbitrary numbers of hits. The story decides.
