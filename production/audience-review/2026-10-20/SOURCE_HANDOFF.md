### Production hypothesis

The opening asks viewers to hold a proposition before demonstrating its consequence. The core sensory contradiction becomes explicit after several seconds, while the visible heat-transfer explanation arrives substantially later.

**Proposed test:** communicate the contradiction in the opening frame or first spoken sentence and begin a purposeful action immediately. An illustrative hand contacting both materials could make the contrast understandable before detailed explanation. Use a clear heat-flow representation when the narration reaches that mechanism.

This is a proposal, not a finding that the existing opening caused abandonment. Avoid presenting conceptual arrows as measured heat maps, or implying that conductivity alone exhausts transient contact physics. The scientific script should check the explanation against primary sources, state its starting and contact conditions, and preserve the distinction between temperature and heat-transfer rate.

No audible narration, music, SFX or complete moving-film review was performed. Do not infer weak sound, missing effects, poor pacing of spoken words or synchronization errors from these frames.

## 9. Proposed audience direction: local entry, scientific depth

The Curio Bureau promise remains: **a reassuring assumption fails, a hidden scientific mechanism explains why, and its consequences become stranger**. Geographic relevance should serve that promise rather than replace it.

PROPOSED: Philippine situations explained in clear English, with mechanisms that also interest people elsewhere. Keep the same narrator and production standard initially. A Tagalog/Taglish test can follow separately; changing subject, language, narrator and visual style together would make interpretation harder.

Two source-backed research directions follow. Neither is selected or approved for production.

### A. Pinatubo's aftermath

- **Question:** “Pinatubo erupted in 1991. Why did towns keep getting buried afterward?”
- **Contradiction:** The explosive event ended, but destructive movement of its deposits continued.
- **Mechanism:** Rain remobilized volcanic deposits into lahars over subsequent rainy seasons. USGS documents major continued lowland destruction.
- **Scientific boundaries:** Distinguish eruption products, ordinary flooding and sediment-rich lahars; do not imply every lahar is a new eruption or turn historical observations into a present-day warning.
- **Visual opportunity:** Show an eruption deposit as stored material, then rainfall and channels redistributing it into lowlands. Keep the mechanism geographically and physically legible.
- **Status:** A researched angle, not an episode commission.

### B. Ground that loses strength

- **Question:** “During an earthquake, how can the ground beneath a building lose its strength?”
- **Local connection:** The 1990 Luzon earthquake and liquefaction observed in places including Dagupan.
- **Contradiction:** Ground that supported a structure can lose supporting strength during shaking.
- **Mechanism:** In susceptible saturated sand, shaking can increase pore-water pressure and reduce effective stress, causing loss of strength.
- **Scientific boundaries:** Not all soil liquefies. Sediment properties, saturation, shaking and drainage matter. Avoid presenting all Philippine sites as equally susceptible.
- **Visual opportunity:** A cutaway showing grains, water and structural support before and during shaking.
- **Status:** A researched angle, not an episode commission.

These choices combine local recognition with universal mechanisms. We have not measured demand, keyword volume or expected views for either.

## 11. Instructions for the production agent

### Before scripting

- Confirm the selected question and intended viewer.
- Verify the mechanism with primary scientific sources and keep uncertainty explicit.
- Write the first-frame promise and the actual explanatory payoff together.
- State what the viewer should understand after the opening, the mechanism and the final resolution.
- Reject a dramatic hook if the science cannot deliver its literal promise.

### Before animation

- Review the storyboard at phone size.
- Ensure the subject and contradiction are readable without studying small decorative labels.
- Make actions change the explanation: contact, transfer, compression, displacement or another mechanism-specific event.
- Prefer purposeful progression followed by readable holds. More transitions alone are not an improvement.
- Budget a meaningful explanatory change approximately every 1.5–2 seconds where the natural narration supports it. This is an editorial safeguard, not a requirement to insert arbitrary cuts.
- Check multipart assets, contact points, directions and labels against the mechanism.

### During moving and sound review

- Inspect the opening at normal speed with the actual audio.
- Align retention timestamps with what is genuinely visible and spoken.
- Check natural-speed Bernard narration, evolving original music and audible synchronized SFX.
- Verify centered captions, scientific caveats and the exact established ending.
- Review the full film and encoded delivery, not only contact sheets or the first scene.
- Record what was actually watched and listened to. Technical audio presence is not a listening result.
