# Owner-supplied retro material direction

Project-level asset direction, 12 October 2026. Compatible with the adopted v1.4 Bible; not a Bible replacement or an automatic approval of a new render.

The owner said the second map test sounded better, but still needed more aesthetic and visually accurate textures. The supplied Indieground packs are a personal style reference and a source material library. Use their actual folds, fibers, paper wear, wrinkles and reflections where these properties fit the pictured object.

## Starting selections after inspection

- **VintagePaper 01 or 03:** first choices for a printed atlas/document base. Color-grade their warm tone into Curio's ivory without destroying surface detail. Treat the image as a finite piece of paper; do not assume it is seamless or invent historic aging.
- **FoldedPaper 02:** candidate for a recognizable folded-map surface. Register folds to the map/document plane and compose them around labels. A fold crossing the center does not justify obscuring the caption.
- **GluedPaper 03:** candidate for restrained pasted-paper structure. Stronger 04/05 wrinkles suit pasted posters more than a clean analytical chart.
- **Plastic 01/02:** first candidates for subtle sleeve reflections; 03/04/05 have more emphatic wrinkles and require a specific reason. Screen/Lighten can remove their dark backing, but the highlights need a mask and a plausible material plane.
- **PhotocopyNoise 01/03:** candidate source for reproduction variation. Normalize levels and mask the treatment into printed material; do not darken the entire composition by default.

These selections are art-direction proposals based on inspected pack images, not a new approved moving test. The catalog records actual resolution and orientation. Full-resolution sources are private under `assets/visual/indieground/local/` after verified import.

## Material responsibilities

**Paper:** source-bound color/fibers/folds travel with the document's camera transform. Use Multiply or a luminance-derived modulation where appropriate. Avoid a screen-locked texture sliding over geography. Use one deliberately cropped sheet; seams and folds have physical meaning.

**Ink:** density variation, edge spread and small dry-ink breaks belong to the printed or drawn layer. A low-opacity blurred duplicate under a crisp core can suggest ink absorption. Keep chart values, geographic contours and text identity intact. Do not displace an actual boundary to simulate a hand-drawn annotation.

**Plastic:** reflections belong to a sleeve or wrap, not to every map, photo and caption. Maintain selective clear areas over important labels. Perspective and movement must agree with the wrapped item.

**Optical finish:** grain, exposure variation and mechanical jitter are separate from the object's material texture. Their seed, update cadence and amplitude must be controlled independently. Keep the logical explanatory camera separate from frame-level film movement. Overlay critical captions after effects if that improves reading.

## Palette and typography

Preserve navy/ivory/gold/coral and Playfair Display/Manrope. The colorful acid, holographic, graffiti, gradient and abstract packs are selective materials for appropriate subjects, not a new default palette. Cutout letters may create a particular evidence/headline treatment; body captions remain coherent and readable.

## Review before adopting a material combination

Compare texture off/on at phone size and native output size. Inspect the camera's intermediate positions: fibers, folds, print and reflections must remain attached. Check the shared focus blur/brightness field, important labels and captions, annotation edge boil and ink bleed separately. A pleasing still does not establish a seamless moving result. Source-material licensing and visual authenticity remain distinct checks.

Do not claim material packs alone create a 100% Vox match. Editorial continuity, composition, information timing and sound association still govern the sequence.
