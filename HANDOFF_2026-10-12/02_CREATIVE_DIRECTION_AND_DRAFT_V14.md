## 1. The central finding
The five reviewed Vox titles differ visibly in art direction. The transferable grammar is not one mandated look, palette, preset, or animation tool. **Art direction + visual reasoning + motion choreography + documentary editing** work together to preserve a recognizable subject while changing the viewer's understanding. Curio should develop original premium mixed-media documentary short-form imagery, with tactile archival collage, clear map/data graphics and constructed mechanical objects as appropriate. It must NOT produce “moving scrapbook posters,” four separate paper cards, rudimentary diagrams, a static art gallery or an endless camera pan pretending to be filmmaking.

### The canonical creative sentence
**Known state → meaningful operation → newly readable state → hold (or next question).**
For each beat ask: “After this changes, what can the viewer understand now that was not apparent before?” If the answer is only “more movement,” stop.
**Continuity of visual thought** is more important than physical continuity of camera space: a real link between adjacent shots can be an object, position, layout, color/category, evidence, geographic location, question, measurement or temporal progression. A clean hard cut is often appropriate. Do not force all shots into a morph.

## 3. Artwork is not equivalent to added texture
Build deliberately composed, subject-specific visual materials:
- recognizable, era-grounded objects/people/environments; genuine licensed documentary pictures or **clearly disclosed illustrative reconstructions**;
- torn archive paper, source-native labels, photograph borders, folds, maps, graph paper, clippings, markers, brush/ink and shadows **only when material role justifies**;
- mixed imagery with purposeful differences in scale and detail; strong hierarchy; one current reading target; reserved future space;
- consistent object-related lighting, overlap, paper contact, edge quality and shadow; source pages must contain actual readable information, not pseudo-archival gibberish;
- genuine historiographic distinction among photograph, source image, infographic, schematic, claim and conjecture.

Do NOT mistake “paper” for “Vox”: ivory background + randomized torn rectangle + big headline + primitive vehicle + generic border is exactly what owner rejects. Flattening every visual into the same brand palette, grain, paper and shadow treatment kills the ability to move between documentary evidence, object inspection, maps, graphs and systems.

### **IMPORTANT USER REFINEMENT #2 (binding to proposed draft)**
Procedural Canvas/SVG is NOT inherently inferior: sophisticated maps, typography, quantitative data, diagrams, vector illustration and graphic motion can be produced programmatically. Evaluate output specificity/craft, not creation method. Deliberately reducing a photographic mechanical subject into a CLEAR analytically precise simplified schematic is legitimate; don't call that automatically a deterioration. It fails when abstracted detail eliminates the distinctive feature the story needs or when the form is a generic placeholder.

## 4. Visual modes and when to use them
**Evidence/observation:** actual source-grounded photograph, footage, document, or accurate drawn reconstruction occupies a large deliberate crop; labels are restrained, attribution intact.

**Object/mechanism:** dimensional and multi-part credible apparatus with parts, contacts, materials and realistic silhouettes when needed; object becomes inspectable, component motion exposes mechanism.

**Geography:** full-frame clear relevant map with source-accurate orientation, dates, markers and valid labels; authored crisp overlay, not decorative fake topographic scribbles. Identify all schematic liberties.

**Comparison/quantitative:** fixed honest common scale, same-size units and clearly defined labels/denominators, correct values and held endpoint.

**Shared archive / 2.5D:** photographer/illustrator purpose-composed material strata, semantic masks, foreground/mid/background with hidden-region reconstruction, controlled camera travel to select meaningful source imagery; not universal side-to-side touring.

A rich archival setup and a clean data diagram need not share the same surface treatment. Visual consistency emerges from typography, semantic color, narration, editorial intelligence and motivated relationship—not all pictures being beige.

## 5. Motion grammar: a director plans THE STORY BEFORE complex visuals
The sequence must establish WHAT survives across shots and WHAT information changes. The very first editorial task is the narrative proposition, not creating a series of wallpaper stills. The shot progression is a hierarchy: initial recognition, structure, relationship, decisive change, conclusion hold. Meaningful movements include isolate, compare, accumulate, reveal, transfer, rotate, annotate, open, combine, reclassify, transform an external view into a cross-section, or cut to a different form of evidence.

### **IMPORTANT USER REFINEMENT #1 (binding to proposed draft)**
**Editorial sequence planning occurs before polished final source artwork.** The original v1.4 draft erroneously placed a detailed scene-direction contract AFTER making artworks; user corrected this. Correct order:
1. Narrative claim, supporting truth/rights research, viewer's mental model and intended revelation.
2. Shot-to-shot relationships, multiple representation choices and WHY, stable anchor(s), transition forms, emotional/cognitive pacing.
3. Three to six initial/intermediate/resolved COMPOSED STATES per difficult shot, motions/attention and reading holds, caption/audio footprints.
4. Asset breakdown for those operations (reconstructed hidden regions, layer topology, key materials, insets, true object dimensions, mask/anchor plans).
5. Purpose-built premium artwork, then visual approval, with planned transition feasible from those assets.
6. Independent masks/planes/local coords/annotations/camera and accurate compositing.
7. Animated proof with rich first/intermediate/final frames, editorials and motion QA; iterate unsatisfactory art.
8. Voice/music/SFX, finished exports and release QA.
Do NOT approve a static scene simply because it is beautiful if its intended transition cannot be animated convincingly.

## 6. Spatial vocabulary
Three source-derived depth systems: **flat analytical page** (stable data coordinate system, grouping), **dimensional object** (surface/cross-section), **archival shared space** (sources physically arranged in layers, perspective, camera select). Choose deliberately. Valid parallax needs foreground, midground, background and cloned/inpainted missing pixels, plus realistic depth relationships; it's a technique, not the brand.

A user point: “you can even make the objects go past the camera to transition to another scene ... it is not always parallax ... learn when to add it.” An occluding object passing the lens can provide a motivated transition, but NOT every boundary must be hidden. Cut straight when changing the evidence mode or historical time avoids false connection.

Use source-local parent transforms for handwritten annotation and map anchors; if camera moves, source-related marks must track their actual corresponding coordinate. Captions remain screen-anchored in the center-safe area. Don't animate chart markers as if their quantitative value is changing when the camera is merely zooming.

## 7. Editing and time
Hold valuable frames long enough to RECOGNIZE the object, INFER the mechanism and READ the labels. “Constant change” is not a license to make all text/type/objects move. One primary action + supporting action is often enough. Words of distinction, dates or values can be highlighted after the source exists; remove obsolete emphasis when meaning changes. Use different creative tasks: inspection, evidence reading, comparison, system construction, surprise/reveal, contemplation.

Transitions chosen by semantic relationship:
- purposeful archive camera navigation, only if sources cohabit a meaningful larger world;
- source image becomes analytically clean redraw maintaining orientation;
- physical object exterior→interior/cross-section;
- peak-speed registered image match cut (tutorial T06), useful only if the cut represents a real logical handoff;
- realistic foreground occlusion (an original Curio option);
- direct cut to a different form of evidence, often strongest;
- handdrawn ink reveal only at correct location, not generic repeated circle.

Do not make the music, motion and typography all demand peak attention simultaneously; sound design includes realistic tactile Foley and audible evolving musical tension but reference-specific exact Vox sound cues are NOT established.

## 9. Information ethics and rights
Every map/route, number, quote, image source, era, body count and claim must be source justified; color category and denominator labelled; fictional imagery clearly reconstructed. Source document must be shown in context before highlight. Never fake a newspaper clipping as genuine evidence. Mixed archival styles should not imply association or proof absent. Historical map changes require date/legend and stable geographic anchor; use cut rather than false geological morph.

## 10. Testing real ART and motion
**Reject** despite technically valid video when: composite looks poster/slideshow/card template; machine/humans generic; movement mechanical; first frames rich but midpoint/end primitive; source detail replaced by noise/texture; camera always drifts; parallax pieces slide inconsistently; animation does not teach; transition paper wipe operates generically; fonts illegible; map invented without labelled schematic status; audio/captions unfinished represented as final.

- viewers can describe one new specific relationship learned from the motion;
- adjacent shots inherit a meaningful visual/thought anchor or explain why the cut occurs;
- annotations/readable evidence remain anchored; correct physical/schematic semantics;
- real video plays naturally and looks beautifully edited at PHONE SIZE without narration; full mix adds clarity;
