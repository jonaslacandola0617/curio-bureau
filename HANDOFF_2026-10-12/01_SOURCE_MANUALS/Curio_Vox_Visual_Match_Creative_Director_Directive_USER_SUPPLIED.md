## 2. Art direction: use four visual modes

### Mode A — Documentary observation

Use an authentic archival image, appropriately licensed footage, or a carefully drawn reconstruction based on a specific historical reference. Let the image fill the frame or occupy a large, deliberate crop. Preserve photographic detail, people, setting, and material cues when they contribute to recognition.

Place one editorial label or one annotation over it. A document/photo may have its own edge; the entire scene does not need a second torn-paper container behind it. Distinguish reconstruction from archival evidence clearly.

### Mode B — Explanatory mechanism

Use a full-frame analytical field. Place the real source of the action in one part of the frame and its destination in another. Build the connecting relationship in sequence. The object, line, label, and endpoint each have a job.

A dark ink field is appropriate for projected sound or radio traffic. Leave deliberate open space between the source and the receiving interpretation. That space is where the mechanism becomes visible.

### Mode C — Geographic context

Use a map large enough to understand, occupying the actual frame rather than a miniature map inside a decorative card. Show only geography and labels relevant to the explanation. The map should orient the viewer or explain positioning; it should not serve as a backdrop for unrelated numerical text.

### Mode D — Quantitative comparison

Use a stable common encoding. Align equal-size units or bars on an explicit baseline. Keep the comparison still while the numbers and relationship are understood. A dramatic camera move must not make a quantity appear to grow.

**For this 16-second rebuild, use all four modes in the sequence below.** Do not normalize them into four matching cards. Unify them through type hierarchy, semantic colors, annotations, and continuity of the argument.

### Timing and curves

- A simple label entry should usually resolve in approximately 0.20–0.35 s in this build.
- A principal reframing move should resolve in approximately 0.55–0.85 s. A useful implementation starting point is an ease-out curve such as `1 - (1-u)^3`, where `u` is normalized 0–1 progress. This is a proposed curve, not a measured reference setting.
- A connecting/path reveal should resolve over approximately 0.40–0.75 s, adjusted to the actual distance and narration cue.
- Allow the viewer to see a finished state. Do not use every remaining frame for a slow drift.
- Avoid spring bounce, continuous floating, perpetual pulsing, and random directional entrances in this version. The reference studies do not establish them as the shared visual language.
- If you choose one moving representation match, register position, scale, direction, and speed before cutting. Use TRN-01’s actual speed peak; do not assume the midpoint. A direct cut remains the preferred solution at the sound/radio boundary.

### Layer construction

Separate these groups in the project: environment/base; documentary source or equipment; mechanism; annotation; primary label; secondary attribution; optional finishing. Use scene-relative coordinates so attached labels and marks survive reframing.

Do not animate the entire hierarchy with the same transform. If the camera reframes, compensate only where intentional screen-fixed text is needed. If the comparison is measuring quantity, hold its camera fixed while the units change.

For code-based production, expose deterministic scene time and named start/end states. Implement path-length reveals, object groups, camera transforms, and unit arrays explicitly. A Canvas/SVG/HTML implementation is an adaptation, so verify its output against these compositions rather than claiming it is an exact After Effects effect.

## 6. Finishing: restraint after composition

The submitted video already contains cream paper, irregular edges, layered shadows, muted equipment, and small editorial labels. Adding more of those treatments would strengthen the current scrapbook look. Finish the rebuilt modes according to their material roles:

- **Paper:** use on an actual document or photo artifact, not around every explanatory scene.
- **Shadows:** use a soft local contact cue where an artifact is presented as physical. Avoid thick floating-card shadows around maps, signal diagrams, and numerical fields.
- **Grain:** one low-strength unified pass if beneficial. Check final-size type and encoded output. Grain cannot supply missing object detail.
- **Chromatic separation:** optional, very slight, and judged at normal playback size. Do not allow colored text fringes to impair reading.
- **Edge boil:** only on hand-drawn annotations, not on the entire tank, document, or data field. Use the Bible’s fine/broad separation and tune to the line width.
- **Temporal sampling:** preserve the current 24 fps delivery unless the project requires another rate. A 12 fps treatment may be tested locally on drawn marks or a collage layer; it is not mandatory across photography, camera movement, maps, and type. Review the completed transition after any stepping treatment.
- **Global jitter/flicker:** optional and very small; omit from precise comparisons if it reduces stability. If using the Bible’s exposure recipe, use its labeled corrected cadence only after testing, rather than copying the unused-variable defect as a reliable rate control.

Keep the final picture varied: documentary detail, a clear mechanism field, geographic orientation, and quantitative structure. Material treatment must not flatten those modes into one universal beige presentation.
